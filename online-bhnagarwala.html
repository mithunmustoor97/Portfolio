The Villain.

Entropy & Information Chaos

Some projects land in your lap with dashboards and design systems. This wasn't one of them.

Online Bhangarwala started as a simple question from the client: "Can we build something that lets users schedule scrap pickups, and agents can manage collections - like Swiggy, but for kabadi?"

The real villain wasn't a person; it was the process. Agents relied on paper scraps and memory. Customers relied on shouting from balconies. This chaos led to:

  • Missed pickups and revenue loss.
  • Zero data visibility for business owners.
  • High cognitive load for low-literacy agents.

Discovery.

Mapping the Chaos

We didn't just guess. Stakeholder interviews revealed that while customers wanted a sleek "Silicon Valley" app, agents often struggled with complex text. We audited competitors like Scrap Uncle and Kabadiwala to find the gaps in onboarding and trust.

The Blueprint.

Clarity > Decorations

We mapped user flows for three distinct personas. The focus was on stripping away non-essential elements to ensure usability for agents who might be new to smartphones.

01

Modular Design System

Created a scalable component library with predefined spacing, typography, and color tokens to ensure consistency across Customer App, Agent App, and Admin Panel.

02

Linear User Flows

Agent workflows were designed as a single, linear path. No branching logic. Step 1 must be completed before seeing Step 2. This prevents errors in the field.

03

Role-Based Access

Admins see high-level data. Agents see only their immediate tasks. Customers see only their pickup status. Information is disclosed progressively.

What I Learned

  • Designing for low-literacy users means eliminating friction completely. Icons > Text.
  • A consistent design system saves massive time for both designers and developers.
  • Collaborating early with developers prevents miscommunication and rework later in the cycle.
